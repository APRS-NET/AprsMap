# F3.4 Two-way IGate: messages from APRS-IS to RF

Status: built for 0.7.0 ([#208](https://github.com/APRS-NET/AprsMap/issues/208)); decisions proposed 2026-10-26 for the
maintainer's review. The receive-only half is [F3.4-rx-igate](F3.4-rx-igate.md).

## User story

As an operator running an IGate, I can also pass messages back: a message on APRS-IS for a station my TNC hears
locally goes out on RF, so a mobile without internet can carry on a conversation with someone on the internet. It
sends only what the rules allow, slowly, and never without Transmit on.

## Background: what already exists

- The receive-only IGate (F3.4-rx-igate): a profile switch gates what its TNCs hear to APRS-IS, held by every transmit
  rule, marked `qAO` (receive only).
- APRS.NET 0.7.0-alpha.1 (G16, APRS.NET#212–#214): `HeardStations` records who is heard on RF, over how many hops and on
  which port, and says who is local; `InternetToRfGate` decides which APRS-IS packets go to RF by the aprs-is.net IGate
  rules and builds the third-party packet (`IGATE>TOCALL,PATH:}SENDER>DEST,TCPIP,IGATE*:…`), with rate limits as Dire
  Wolf's `IGTXLIMIT` (6 a minute, 10 in five minutes), a duplicate window, and the sender's next position sent once
  after a message.
- APRS-IS servers send a station's messages to the IGate that last gated a packet from it, unless that IGate is receive
  only.

## Behavior

1. **Pass messages to RF** is a second switch on the IGate card in Settings › Sources, for the active profile, **off by
   default**, and only with the IGate on. With it on, what's gated to APRS-IS is no longer marked receive only (`qAR`
   rather than `qAO`), so APRS-IS sends the IGate messages for the stations it hears.
2. **Who is local**: every packet the profile's TNCs hear is recorded with its hops and the TNC that heard it (the
   station's own packets aren't). A station heard directly or over up to 2 hops in the last 3 hours is local
   (APRS.NET's defaults, as Dire Wolf's IGate).
3. **What goes to RF** (APRS.NET's rules): messages, acknowledgements and rejections for a local station, unless the
   sender is local too (the addressee hears it directly) or the path says `TCPXX`, `NOGATE` or `RFONLY`; after a
   message, the sender's next position, once, so the addressee sees who's writing. Never bulletins, announcements or
   queries, and never a packet that came from RF.
4. **Where**: on the TNC that heard the addressee, as a third-party packet from the station's callsign, with its
   TOCALL and that TNC's transmit path.
5. **Slowly**: at most 6 packets a minute and 10 in five minutes on each TNC; more are refused, not queued. A copy
   within 30 seconds is refused.
6. **Held** like everything the station sends: with Transmit off, a profile that may not send, no verified APRS-IS
   login, or a TNC that's receive only, nothing goes to RF, and the card says why.
7. **Counts and the heard list**: the card adds how many packets went to RF and how many the rules kept back since the
   app started; the dashboard gets a **Heard on RF** tile with the stations heard on the TNCs, most recent first, their
   hops and when, each a link that selects the station.

## Service surface

- `StationProfile.IGateToRf` (`bool?`, off unless set); `EffectiveIGateToRf` needs `EffectiveIGate` too.
- `IGateStatus.ToRf`, `GatedToRf`, `RefusedToRf`.
- `Dashboard.HeardOnRf` (`DashboardHeard(Station, Hops, LastHeard)`, most recent first, up to 10) and
  `Dashboard.HeardOnRfTotal`.

## APRS.NET dependencies

G16, delivered in 0.7.0-alpha.1 (`HeardStations`, `InternetToRfGate`, `RfGateDecision`).

## Desktop vs web differences

None in behavior. On the gateway the switch is the operator's (F2.9), like the receive-only one.

## Acceptance tests

Every test that transmits uses a fake TNC or a local Dire Wolf with null audio, never a radio.

1. A message on APRS-IS for a station heard on the TNC goes out on that TNC as a third-party packet; the sender's next
   position follows once.
2. A message for a station not heard, or heard over 3 hops, or whose sender is heard on RF, isn't sent; nor a
   bulletin.
3. Nothing goes to RF with the switch off, with Transmit off, or on a receive-only TNC; with it on, gated packets carry
   `qAR`, with it off `qAO`.
4. The seventh packet in a minute is refused.
5. The card counts what went to RF and what didn't; the dashboard lists the stations heard.

## Decisions for review

1. **Off by default, and only with the IGate on** (proposed).
2. **APRS.NET's rules and Dire Wolf's limits as they are** (proposed): 2 hops, 3 hours, 6 a minute and 10 in five.
3. **One gate a TNC** (proposed), each with its own transmit path and limits, sharing who's heard; a message goes out on
   the TNC that heard its addressee.
4. **The heard list on the dashboard** (proposed), since that's where the station's numbers are; Details could show
   "heard here over 1 hop" later.
