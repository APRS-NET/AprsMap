# F2.13 Ping: a remote connectivity check

Status: spec'd for 0.6.0 ([#307](https://github.com/APRS-NET/AprsMap/issues/307)), decisions agreed 2026-10-19 (maintainer request). Not built.

## User story

As an operator away from my station, I send it a message beginning "AprsMap ping" from my handheld or any APRS client,
and it answers "AprsMap pong" with the time, the same way the ping came. So I know the station is running, hearing and
able to send, without anyone at home. I choose who may ping it and how often it may answer, and it's off until I turn
it on.

## Background: what already exists

- Messaging (F2.4): conversations with acks and retries, the source a message came on, and every send following F2.1's
  rules (licensed profile, the Transmit switch, sources not RX only).
- Not yet built, planned for 0.6.0: an away auto-reply (F2.10) and answers to APRS queries such as `?APRSP` (F2.8). This
  feature is neither: it answers one phrase the operator chose, with the time.

## Behavior

### Turning it on

1. **Answer pings** is a switch in Settings › Messaging, **off** for every new and existing profile (maintainer,
   2026-10-19): the pong is sent on the air by itself, so the operator chooses it, as with beacons.
2. Pongs follow every rule a message does (F2.1): a licensed profile, the Transmit switch on, and the source not RX only
   and switched on. While any of these stops it, a ping is shown (behavior 11) and not answered, and the reason is
   logged.

### What's a ping

3. **The ping phrase** and **the pong phrase** are set in Settings › Messaging. Their defaults are built from the
   product's name (`Brand.DisplayName`): "{name} ping" and "{name} pong", so "AprsMap ping" and "AprsMap pong" today. Each
   is 1 to 40 printable ASCII characters, without `|`, `~` or `{` (F2.4's message rules), and they can't be the same.
4. **A message is a ping when it starts with the ping phrase** (maintainer, 2026-10-19): compared ignoring case
   (`OrdinalIgnoreCase`: character by character, no language rules), after trimming spaces at both ends. "AprsMap ping",
   "aprsmap PING" and "AprsMap ping from the car" are pings; "did my AprsMap ping work?" isn't.
5. Only messages **addressed to the profile's callsign** count, not bulletins, announcements, group messages (F2.5) or
   messages to other stations heard; nor a message that is itself a pong, so two stations never answer each other.

### Who may ping

6. **Who may ping**, a choice in Settings › Messaging (maintainer, 2026-10-19):
   1. **Anyone** (the default once it's on);
   2. **My callsign, any SSID**: the profile's base callsign with any SSID (NZ0P-10 answers NZ0P-9 and NZ0P);
   3. **These callsigns**: a list the operator types, one callsign a line, an entry without an SSID matching any SSID of
      that callsign, and `*` at the end matching any suffix (`WA0RC*`).
   A ping from anyone else is shown (behavior 11) but not answered.

### How often

7. **At most one pong every N seconds**, for the whole station, **N set in Settings, 60 by default and never less than
   10** (maintainer, 2026-10-19). A ping that comes within the interval is shown and not answered; it's logged as
   "too soon". Messages' own acks are not pongs, and are sent as usual.

### The pong

8. **The pong** is the pong phrase, a space and the time in UTC with the date: `AprsMap pong 2026-10-19 03:12:45Z`
   (maintainer, 2026-10-19). At most 67 characters, the most one message holds: a pong phrase of 40 characters still
   fits.
9. **It goes back the way the ping came** (maintainer, 2026-10-19): a ping heard on a TNC is answered on that TNC with
   its TX path, one from APRS-IS over APRS-IS, so a pong also proves the path the ping took. The conversation's Send via
   is not used. If that source can't send now (behavior 2), it isn't answered another way.
10. The pong is a message like any other: it has a message ID, is acked by the sender's station, and is retried by
    F2.4's schedule for that source (APRS-IS or Default).

### Seen in the app

11. **Pings and pongs show in the station's conversation** (maintainer, 2026-10-19), the pong marked "automatic reply".
    A ping doesn't count as unread, raise a notice or show the unread marker. A ping that wasn't answered says why
    ("not answered: Transmit is off", "not from a callsign that may ping", "too soon").
12. The log has a line for each ping: who, on which source, and answered or why not.

## Service surface

- `PingSettings(bool On = false, string? Ping = null, string? Pong = null, PingWho Who = Anyone,
  IReadOnlyList<string>? Callsigns = null, int IntervalSeconds = 60)` on `StationProfile` (`Ping`); null phrases mean the
  defaults of behavior 3. `PingWho`: `Anyone`, `MyCallsign`, `Callsigns`.
- `ChatMessage` gains `Automatic` (a pong) and `Ping` (a ping, with `PingResult`: `Answered`, `Off`, `NotAllowed`,
  `TooSoon`, `CantSend`), so the UI can mark them and leave them out of the unread count.
- Error codes: `settings.ping.phrase-invalid`, `settings.ping.phrases-same`, `settings.ping.interval-too-short`,
  `settings.ping.callsign-invalid`.
- The engine's messenger (F2.4) answers pings; no new `IEngineService` member beyond the settings.

## APRS.NET dependencies

None beyond G10 (messaging, delivered): matching a phrase the operator chose and stamping the time are the app's. The
message rules (length, characters) are APRS.NET's `AprsMessage` validation, as for any message.

## Desktop vs web differences

| | Desktop | Web (gateway) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Turning it on, the phrases, who, how often | The user | The operator (F2.9) |
| Answering | While the app runs | While the gateway runs, which suits an always-on station |

## Acceptance tests

1. Off (the default), a ping is shown, not answered, and says why; nothing is sent.
2. On, "aprsmap ping from the car" from NZ0P-9 heard on the TNC is answered on that TNC, with its TX path, by
   "AprsMap pong 2026-10-19 03:12:45Z" (a fake clock); a ping over APRS-IS is answered over APRS-IS.
3. "did my AprsMap ping work?" and a bulletin starting with the phrase aren't pings; a pong isn't answered.
4. Who may ping: My callsign answers NZ0P-9 and not N0ABC; These callsigns with `WA0RC*` answers WA0RC-7.
5. Two pings 5 s apart with a 10 s interval: the first is answered, the second shown as "too soon"; an interval under
   10 is refused with `settings.ping.interval-too-short`.
6. With Transmit off or the source RX only, a ping is shown "not answered" with the reason, and nothing is sent.
7. Changing the phrases is honoured at once; a phrase with `{` or over 40 characters is refused.
8. A ping doesn't change the unread count; the pong shows "automatic reply" and becomes Delivered when acked.
9. On a gateway, a viewer can't change the ping settings (`gateway.operator-only`).

## Decisions

Agreed with the maintainer (2026-10-19):

1. **Off by default**, one switch in Settings › Messaging.
2. **Who may ping** is a choice: anyone, my callsign with any SSID, or a list of callsigns.
3. **How often** is a setting for the whole station, 60 s by default, never less than 10 s.
4. **Starts with** the ping phrase, ignoring case (`OrdinalIgnoreCase`) and spaces at either end.
5. **UTC with the date** after the pong phrase.
6. **Back the way the ping came**, not by Send via.
7. **Shown in the conversation**, not counted as unread.
8. **The settings are per profile**, like the rest of a profile's messaging, so a club call can have its own.
9. **The interval is for the whole station**, not per sender: several stations pinging at once still get at most one
   pong every N seconds.
