# F3.6 GPS

Status: built for 0.6.0 ([#205](https://github.com/APRS-NET/AprsMap/issues/205)) except the OS location API, browser
geolocation and a tracked position (decision 6); decisions proposed 2026-10-21 for the maintainer's review. In the UI:
Settings › Sources › GPS; its pill in the status bar.

## User story

As a mobile operator, I plug in a GPS (or point the app at gpsd, or at a GPS shared over the network), and my station
is where the GPS says: my beacons carry my position, course and speed, my APRS-IS range follows me, and the map's
house is where I am. When the GPS loses its fix, the app says so and falls back to my profile's fixed location.

## Background: what already exists

- Sources (F1.25): a master list of sources, picked per profile; each runs, reconnects with backoff and has a pill in
  the status bar. F1.25 behavior 12 sketched a GPS source with **Position for**.
- APRS.NET 0.5.0-alpha.1: `NmeaSentence` and `NmeaFixTracker` (G17) turn NMEA lines into a `GpsFix` (position, time,
  speed, course, altitude, satellites, HDOP, 2D or 3D fix); `AprsNet.Gps`'s `GpsdConnection` (G30) reads gpsd and gives
  the same `GpsFix`, reconnecting by itself. Reading a serial port or a socket stays in the app (G17).
- The Source console (F1.28) shows each source's traffic.

## Behavior

### Adding a GPS

1. **GPS** is a source kind in Settings › Sources, with three ways to reach it (proposed):
   1. **NMEA over serial**: a port and its speed, **4800** baud by default (the NMEA 0183 rate; many USB GPSs use
      9600, offered beside it);
   2. **NMEA over TCP**: a host and port, **10110** by default (the port commonly used for NMEA over a network);
   3. **gpsd**: a host and port, **2947** by default, on this machine or another.
2. A profile uses at most **one GPS** (proposed); a second is refused when saving, saying why.
3. A GPS never sends; it's RX only by nature (F2.1), so no Transmit choices show for it.

### Its state

4. Its pill in the status bar says how it's doing: connecting or reconnecting as other sources do, then the fix:
   **No fix**, **2D fix** or **3D fix**, with the satellites used where known ("GPS · 3D fix · 9 satellites").
5. A fix is **fresh** for **60 seconds** (proposed). Without a fresh fix the pill says **GPS: no fix, using the
   profile's location** when the profile has one.
6. NMEA lines read from serial or TCP go to the Source console (F1.28), each line once, so they can be watched and
   saved as text. gpsd's reports aren't shown there (proposed: later, with F3.12).

### Where the station is

7. **Position for my station** (F1.25 behavior 12): while the profile's GPS has a fresh fix, the station is where the
   GPS says, in everything that uses the station's location (F1.13):
   1. **Beacons and Send position now** (F2.2, F2.1) send the GPS position, with **course and speed**, and the
      altitude with a 3D fix;
   2. **The APRS-IS filter's range** (F1.2) is around the GPS position, re-sent when the station has moved more than
      a quarter of the range since the last one (proposed), at most once per filter interval as now;
   3. **The map's house and Home** (F1.13, F1.4) are at the GPS position; distances and bearings from me follow.
8. Without a fresh fix, all of these use the profile's fixed location, as without a GPS. The fixed location isn't
   changed by the GPS (proposed): it's the profile's setting, kept for when there's no fix.
9. The position confirmation (F2.2 behavior 12) is unchanged: beacons wait for a position sent by hand once.

### Later

10. **The OS location API** (desktop) and **browser geolocation** sent to the gateway (proposed: a follow-up issue),
    and **a tracked position** (F1.25 behavior 12's second option: a GPS somewhere else shown as a named marker,
    beaconed as an object). The **Moving** beacon schedule, from the GPS's speed and course, is F2.2's revision
    ([#335](https://github.com/APRS-NET/AprsMap/issues/335)); the sky view and accuracy are F3.12
    ([#331](https://github.com/APRS-NET/AprsMap/issues/331)).

## Service surface

- `GpsLink` (`Serial`, `Tcp`, `Gpsd`) and `SourceSettings.GpsLink`; a serial GPS uses `SerialPort` and `Baud`, a TCP
  GPS or gpsd `Host` and `Port`. `SourceSettings.GpsFor…` makes each.
- `EngineStatus.Gps` (`GpsStatus`): the GPS source, its fix kind, position, altitude, speed, course, satellites, HDOP,
  when the fix was taken, and whether it's fresh.
- Error codes: `settings.source.gps-invalid` (no port or host), `settings.profile.two-gps`.

## APRS.NET dependencies

G17 (NMEA parsing, delivered in 0.4.0-alpha.12) and G30 (gpsd client, `AprsNet.Gps`, delivered in 0.4.0-alpha.12).

## Desktop vs web differences

| | Desktop | Web (gateway) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where a serial GPS plugs in | This computer | The gateway's host (a container needs the device passed through) |
| Seeing the fix | The user | Everyone; changing the source is the operator's |

## Acceptance tests

1. NMEA lines (RMC, GGA, GSA) over TCP give a 3D fix with position, speed, course, altitude and satellites in the
   status; the pill says "3D fix · 9 satellites"; the lines are in the console.
2. A fix older than 60 s isn't fresh, and the station falls back to the profile's location.
3. With a fresh fix, a beacon goes out at the GPS position with course and speed; without one, at the profile's.
4. Moving the GPS more than a quarter of the range re-sends the filter around the new position; less doesn't.
5. gpsd's fixes do the same.
6. A profile with two GPS sources, or a GPS without its port or host, is refused.
7. Settings › Sources offers GPS with its three links, and shows the right fields for each.

## Decisions for review

All proposed (2026-10-21), built as described so they can be tried:

1. **Three links**: NMEA over serial (4800 baud), NMEA over TCP (port 10110), gpsd (port 2947).
2. **One GPS per profile.**
3. **A fix is fresh for 60 s**, then the profile's location is used.
4. **The GPS doesn't overwrite the profile's location**; it's used while fresh.
5. **The filter follows the GPS** after moving a quarter of the range.
6. **Later**: the OS location API, browser geolocation and a tracked position; gpsd's raw reports in the console.
