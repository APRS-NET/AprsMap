# F3.14 GPS statistics on the dashboard

Status: built for 0.7.0 ([#382](https://github.com/APRS-NET/AprsMap/issues/382), maintainer request, 2026-10-25); decisions proposed for the maintainer's review. Per-connection
metrics wait for APRS.NET#328 (G37).

## User story

As an operator with a GPS, I see on the Dashboard how my GPS has done over the last hour, 6 hours or day: how much of
the time it had a fix and how often it lost it, how many satellites of each system it used, how the dilutions of
precision and its own error estimates moved, and how far its position wandered while the station stood still, so I can
tell a receiver or antenna problem from a bad sky, and compare one with another. The same numbers go out as
OpenTelemetry metrics, so a long-running station can be watched from a monitoring system.

## Background: what already exists

- GPS sources (F3.6), a radio's GPS on its TNC port (F3.13) and the GPS in use among them (F3.13 behavior 4).
- The GPS panel (F3.12): the fix, accuracy (estimated errors, PDOP, HDOP, VDOP, satellites used of those in view), the
  sky view and the satellite list, all as they are now. `EngineStatus.Gps` carries them.
- The Dashboard (F1.22): a range of 1 hour, 6 hours or 24 hours with points of 1, 5 or 15 minutes, tiles with a big
  number, a line under it, a sparkline with hover, keyboard and Copy, refreshed every 10 seconds while shown.
- APRS.NET's `GpsQuality` also gives TDOP, GDOP and the latitude and longitude errors, which the engine doesn't keep yet.
- The engine's metrics (`AprsMap.Core` meter: packets, rejected lines, connection failures), exported over OTLP when
  `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT` is set (README, Telemetry).

The trigger: a receiver whose firmware mishandled the GPS week rollover lost its fix every minute or so for about 16
seconds, and showed BeiDou satellites flipping between used and not used (maintainer observation, 2026-10-25). The pill
and the panel show only the moment; nothing showed that it kept happening.

## Behavior

### What's counted

1. **The engine samples the GPS in use once a second** (F3.13 behavior 4) while the active profile has a GPS, and keeps
   24 hours of it a minute at a time, like the dashboard's other history (F1.22 "Engine"). Each sample is one of:
   1. **3D fix** or **2D fix**: the GPS in use has a fresh fix (F3.6, 60 s) with a position;
   2. **no fix**: it's reporting, but without a position (or the GPS says it has none);
   3. **silent**: nothing from it for 5 seconds (proposed), such as gpsd unreachable or a GPS unplugged.
   With no GPS in the profile nothing is counted, and the time isn't shown as "no fix".
2. **A fix loss** is a sample of no fix or silent straight after one with a fix; the **outage** lasts until the next
   fix. The engine keeps the outages of the last 24 hours (at most 1,000, the oldest dropped).
3. **Each minute keeps**, over its samples with a fix: the satellites used and in view, overall and for each system
   (GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, BeiDou, QZSS, NavIC, SBAS), as averages; PDOP, HDOP, VDOP, TDOP and GDOP; the estimated
   horizontal, vertical, latitude and longitude errors; and the average signal (dB-Hz) of the satellites used. Each
   as its average, lowest and highest, so a range's figures come from the minutes in it.
4. **Wander** (proposed): while the GPS says the station is still (under 1 knot), each fix's position is kept as an
   offset in metres from the first, so the spread can be worked out: the radius holding 95% of fixes, taken as twice
   the root-mean-square distance from their average (2DRMS). A station that moves isn't counted while it moves.
5. **A different GPS in use** (a radio standing in, F3.13) carries on the same history; the tile says which GPS was in
   use for most of the range, and that another was used for part of it.
6. **Clear packets** (F1.19) doesn't clear GPS history. It's kept across restarts with the dashboard's
   ([#386](https://github.com/APRS-NET/AprsMap/issues/386), F1.22 decision 3); an outage going on when the app stopped
   ends there, since what happened while it was down isn't known, and "now" waits for the first new sample.

### On the Dashboard

7. **A GPS group of four tiles** under a **GPS** heading, after the other tiles and before the tables, shown when the
   active profile has a GPS or had one in the range; the heading names the GPS. Without one, the heading says there's
   no GPS and how to add one (Settings › Sources). Each tile works like
   the others (F1.22 behaviors 4, 6, 8): title, big number, detail, sparkline with hover and keyboard, Copy, read as one
   sentence. Values are in the viewer's units (metres or feet, F0.6).
   1. **GPS fix**: the share of the range with a fix (3D or 2D) as the big number; under it 3D, 2D, no fix and silent
      as shares and times ("3D 97% · 23 h 18 min"), the fix losses in the range, the longest outage, and when the last
      one began and how long it lasted. The sparkline is the share with a fix a point.
   2. **Satellites**: used of those in view now as the big number ("14 of 22"); the average, lowest and highest used
      over the range; then a row for each system seen in the range: used and in view now, and the average used over the
      range. The sparkline is the average used a point.
   3. **Dilution of precision**: HDOP now as the big number; a row each for PDOP, HDOP, VDOP, TDOP and GDOP (those the
      GPS gives) with now, the average and the worst over the range; the F3.12 hint (lower is better; below 2 is good,
      above 5 is poor). The sparkline is the average HDOP a point.
   4. **Accuracy**: the estimated horizontal error now as the big number ("± 2.1 m"); rows for horizontal, vertical,
      latitude and longitude error with now, average and worst; the average signal of the satellites used; and the
      wander while still (behavior 4), with how many fixes it's from. A note says the GPS's own estimates differ by
      source (gpsd at 95%, NMEA GST at one standard deviation), from APRS.NET's documentation. The sparkline is the
      average horizontal error a point.
8. **Gaps say so**: a point with no samples is empty in the sparkline, not zero; a value the GPS never gave is left
   out rather than shown as 0 or "–".
   A value the receiver never reported in the range (a constellation with no satellites, one of the DOPs, the error
   estimates) has no row, and a tile with nothing reported isn't shown; the expanded plot's readout gives each value
   its unit ("2.3 m", "9 satellites", "HDOP 1.1", "92 %"), in the viewer's units (#430).
9. **My view** (F1.22 behavior 7) doesn't change the GPS tiles: they're the station's own.

### OpenTelemetry

10. **The GPS in use, on the engine's meter** (`AprsMap.Core`), recorded from the same samples while the profile has a
    GPS:
    1. `aprsmap.gps.fix.time` (counter, seconds), tagged `fix` = `3d`, `2d`, `none` or `silent`: its rate gives the
       share of time in each;
    2. `aprsmap.gps.fix.losses` (counter, `{loss}`).
    These are the station's: they follow the GPS in use as it changes (F3.13), and count silence, which a connection
    can't. No tag carries a position, a callsign or a source's name or address. The README's telemetry table lists
    them.
11. **Each GPS connection's own numbers come from APRS.NET** (G37, maintainer, 2026-10-25): satellites by
    constellation, the dilutions, the error estimates and signal, as `aprsnet.*` metrics that AprsMap's OTLP export
    already passes on (`AprsNet.*` meters). Until G37 ships they're on the dashboard only.

## Service surface

- `Dashboard.Gps` (new, `DashboardGps?`): null with no GPS in the range; otherwise the GPS in use (its source id, and
  whether another stood in), the fix shares and times, the losses with the longest and last outage, satellites now and
  over the range overall and by system, the dilutions and errors as now, average, lowest and highest, the average
  signal, the wander and its fix count, and a series a point for the sparklines (share with a fix, satellites used,
  HDOP, horizontal error), with null for an empty point.
- `GpsAccuracy` gains `Tdop`, `Gdop`, `LatitudeErrorMeters` and `LongitudeErrorMeters`, from APRS.NET's `GpsQuality`.
- No new `IEngineService` member: `GetDashboardAsync` carries it, so the desktop and the web (SignalR) are the same.

## APRS.NET dependencies

`GpsQuality` and `GpsSatellite` from 0.5.0-alpha.2 (G17, G30) for the dashboard, which is the app's own session
history. **G37** (GPS quality metrics) for each connection's metrics (behavior 11); nothing here waits for it.

## Desktop vs web differences

None. A web viewer sees the station's GPS tiles; with "Hide my location" or "Grid only" (F1.35 behavior 5) the wander
stays, since it's a size and not a position.

## Acceptance tests

1. A GPS that has a 3D fix for 50 minutes and none for 10, sampled with a fake clock, gives 83% with a fix in the last
   hour, one loss, a 10-minute longest outage, and the times of each state.
2. A GPS that stops reporting counts as silent after 5 seconds, and its return with a fix ends the outage.
3. Satellites used by system: two GPS and one BeiDou used, five in view, give those counts now and as averages; a
   system never seen has no row.
4. The dilutions and errors give now, average, lowest and highest over the minutes of the range; a value never given is
   absent.
5. Wander: fixes scattered about a point while still give the expected 2DRMS; fixes while moving aren't counted.
6. No GPS in the profile: `Dashboard.Gps` is null and the tile says how to add one.
7. Metrics: with a `MeterListener`, a second of 3D fix adds a second to `aprsmap.gps.fix.time{fix=3d}`, silence adds to
   `{fix=silent}`, and a loss adds one to `aprsmap.gps.fix.losses`.
8. UI (headless): the four tiles show the numbers above in metric and imperial, read as sentences, Copy gives their
   series, and they fit at 200% text in every theme.

## Decisions for review

1. **Silent after 5 seconds** without a report (proposed): GPSs report once a second, and gpsd's SKY a little less
   often.
2. **Wander only while still**, under 1 knot by the GPS's speed, as 2DRMS (proposed).
3. **One history for the GPS in use**, not one per GPS source (proposed): the dashboard is about the station.
4. **Where the tiles go**: a GPS heading and four tiles after the other tiles, before the tables (proposed): a heading
   in the middle of the wrapping tiles would break their flow.
5. **Metrics tagged without the source** (proposed): a profile has one GPS in use, and source ids aren't meaningful in a
   monitoring system.
6. **Per-connection metrics in APRS.NET** (maintainer, 2026-10-25): AprsMap exports only the GPS in use's fix time and
   losses; satellites, dilutions and errors per connection are G37's.
