# F1.41 Persistent history

Status: in progress for 0.8.0 ([#314](https://github.com/APRS-NET/AprsMap/issues/314), maintainer request, 2026-10-27);
decisions 1–6 by the maintainer, the rest proposed. Built: the store, rotation, the size warning in Settings, the
settings and the configuration (behaviors 1–6, 12, 13), the restart (7), long tracks and kept packets (8, 9), the
dashboard's 7 days (10, 11), the Connection tile's note (5).

## User story

As a station's operator, I come back to the map after a restart or an upgrade and see the stations and tracks it had,
not an empty map. I open a station I've watched all week and see where it went over the last 7 days, and the dashboard
shows the last week of activity, not only the hours since the gateway last restarted. I choose how many days are kept
and how much disk that may take, and I'm told when the disk limit, not the days, is what's cutting the history short.

## Background: what already exists

- The station store (F1.3) and tracks (F1.5) are in memory only: up to 50,000 stations, tracks of up to 500 positions
  and 2 hours, removed after `RemoveAfter`. A restart starts with an empty map.
- The dashboard (F1.22) keeps per-minute counters for 24 hours, saved whole to `dashboard-history.json` every 5
  minutes and as the engine stops (#386), behind `IHistoryStore` so a database can take the file's place (F1.22
  decision for review on storage). Its ranges are 1 hour, 6 hours and 24 hours.
- Packet capture (F1.27) keeps one file a day of problem packets, with age and size limits, the oldest days deleted
  first; reading scans the files.
- `StationStore.Apply` takes each packet's raw bytes and its received time, so packets can be applied again later, in
  order, as they were received.
- Only the engine writes the data folder: on the desktop its own engine, on a gateway the gateway's. Viewers, browsers
  and the desktop as a gateway's client (F1.39) never touch it, so sessions connecting and disconnecting don't affect
  the store.

## Behavior

1. **Every packet received is kept** for the history's length (decision 1): its received time, the source it came
   from (F1.25), the raw packet as received, and, to find it again quickly, the station (or object or item) it's about,
   its kind and its position, if any. Stations and tracks are rebuilt from the packets, so there's one record and no
   second copy to drift. Packets the station transmits are kept as received ones are, marked as its own. What's kept
   is what the packet list lists: a copy on the same source is kept, marked as a duplicate (F1.8 behavior 2), and a
   copy first heard on another source only adds to Seen on (F1.25 behavior 9), so it isn't kept again.
2. **Settings › Data › History** (engine-wide; on a gateway the operator's), beside packet capture, which keeps its
   own limits the same way: **Keep packet history** on or off, **Keep for** 1–30 days, default **7**; **Use at most**
   100–102,400 MB, default **1,024 MB** (decision 2). The gateway's configuration can set both (`[engine.history]
   days = 7`, `max_megabytes = 1024`, `days = 0` for off), and wins on every start, as the configured devices do
   (#424); Settings then shows them without letting them be changed. The file's values are brought into range, as
   Settings would refuse them. Off keeps today's behaviour: memory only, and the history kept so far is deleted after a confirmation.
3. **The section says what's kept**: "7 days kept, 412 MB, since 21/10/2026 14:02", and how full the size limit is.
4. **One file a day** (UTC), `history/2026-10-27.db` in the data folder, so rotating is deleting whole files: no
   rewriting, no pause. Once a minute (with the station sweep) and when the settings change, the engine deletes the days older than **Keep history for**, then
   the oldest days until the files fit **Use at most**. The day being written counts towards the limit.
5. **When the size limit cuts it short** (decision 3): if the limit leaves fewer days than **Keep history for**, the
   History section says so in its warning colour ("The size limit keeps 3 of the 7 days"), the dashboard's Connection
   tile notes it, and the gateway logs it once a day. If even today's file reaches the limit, packets stop being kept
   for the rest of the day (the map and dashboard carry on as now), and the warning says that.
6. **Writing never slows receiving**: packets go to a background writer that commits about once a second. A crash or
   power cut loses at most that last second; each commit is whole or not there at all.
7. **After a restart or an upgrade**, before the sources connect, the engine applies again the packets kept from the
   last `RemoveAfter` (2 hours by default; 24 hours when stations are never removed), in the order and with the times
   they were received, so the map, the station list, the packet list and the tracks come back as they were. Applying
   them again does nothing else: no messages, acks, alerts, notifications, digipeating, IGating, transmitting,
   dashboard counts or keeping them again. The log says how many were put back ("Restored 1,240 packets from the last
   120 minutes"); it runs before the sources connect, so there's nothing yet to show it on.
8. **A station's longer track**, in its details (F1.6): **Track: Live · 24 hours · 7 days**, drawn on the map in place
   of that station's live track (F1.5), from the kept positions with F1.5's 20 m rule and thinned evenly to at most
   10,000 points; new positions join it as they arrive. The choice is per viewer and goes back to Live when another
   station is shown. Without a history, the longer choices show the live track.
9. **A station's kept packets**, in its details: **Show kept packets** lists the newest 100 with their times, and
   **Older packets** loads the next 100, back to the history's start; "No packets are kept for this station." when
   there are none. On a gateway, a viewer the home's location is hidden from (F1.26 behavior 11) gets neither the
   home station's longer track nor its kept packets.
10. **The dashboard over a week** (decision 4): a **7 days** range beside 1 hour, 6 hours and 24 hours, with a point an
    hour, each labelled with its day and time. The minutes stay in `dashboard-history.json` (#386) for the 24 hours;
    each hour, once it has ended, is written to `history/metrics.db`, apart from the packets, and kept **90 days**
    (decision 5) whatever the packet history's length or size limit, as hours are small (about 2 MB for 90 days). The
    stations heard total and the busiest stations over 7 days come from the kept packets, worked out at most every 5
    minutes; where fewer days of packets are kept, or none, a note under the range says how far back they reach ("…cover
    the last 2 days: that's all the history kept"). The busiest relays, the newest stations, the farthest heard and the
    weather stay the minutes' 24 hours. **My view** isn't offered over 7 days, as the per-station counts cover 24
    hours: the range shows the whole feed, and says so. Clearing the packets (F1.19) clears the hours too, as it clears
    the dashboard's counts.
11. **Upgrading keeps the dashboard's history**: on every start, the hours the minutes hold, from
    `dashboard-history.json`, are written to `metrics.db`, so the first start with this feature keeps the 24 hours
    counted before it.
12. **Start over** (F0.10) deletes the history with the rest of what the station has collected, and **Clear stations**
    (F1.19) doesn't: it clears the map, not what was received.
13. **A history file that can't be read** is renamed `.invalid` and left out, as the other stores do, and the warning
    says which day is missing. A history already open in another process (two engines pointed at one data folder) is
    left alone: history is off for that run, and the status says why.

## Service surface

- `AppSettings.History` (`HistorySettings(Days, MaxMegabytes)`, null for the defaults), saved with the other settings;
  `settings.history-invalid` when out of range.
- `IEngineService.GetHistoryStatusAsync()` → `HistoryStatus(Available, Settings, Configured, Bytes, KeptSince,
  DaysKept, Problem, DaysFit, SetAside)` (operator only on a gateway).
- `IEngineService.GetTrackAsync(string name, DateTimeOffset since)` → the kept positions, joined and thinned
  (`TrackPoint.MaxLongTrack`), or the live track without a history (behavior 8).
- `IEngineService.GetStationPacketsAsync(string name, DateTimeOffset? before, int count)` → kept packets, newest
  first, at most 500 a call over the hub (behavior 9).
- `DashboardRange.Week` for `GetDashboardAsync` (behavior 10).
- Core: `PacketHistory` (append, read, rotate, status) in the `history` folder named by `EngineOptions.History.Folder`;
  none for an engine without a data folder. `IMetricsStore` takes `IHistoryStore`'s place with the dashboard.

## APRS.NET dependencies

None new: applying kept packets again uses the decoding the engine already uses. Storage is the app's, not protocol
analysis.

## Desktop vs web differences

None in behaviour. The desktop's own engine keeps its history in its data folder; a gateway keeps it in its own
(`/var/lib/aprsmap/history` for the Linux service, the data volume in the container). The web app, and the desktop as
a gateway's client, show the gateway's history and keep none.

## Implementation notes (proposed)

- SQLite through `Microsoft.Data.Sqlite` with `SQLitePCLRaw.bundle_e_sqlite3` (MIT and Apache-2.0, so allowed in the
  MIT `Core`), which carries the native library for linux-x64, linux-arm64, linux-musl, win-x64 and osx. WAL journal,
  `synchronous=NORMAL`, one writer, no connection pooling (a day's file is deleted whole). A `.lock` file held open
  for the engine's life keeps a second engine out (behavior 13).
- The "about N days fit" estimate is the size limit over the average whole day kept, measured before the limit trims
  them.
- `packets(received_at INTEGER, source TEXT, own INTEGER, duplicate INTEGER, name TEXT, kind INTEGER, lat REAL, lon
  REAL, raw BLOB)` with
  an index on `(name, received_at)`; rows are in received order, so a time range is a rowid range.
- A query over several days opens the day files it needs, newest first, and stops when it has enough.
- Sizes (estimates to check): a filtered feed of 1–5 packets a second is roughly 10–40 MB a day; the full APRS-IS feed
  a few hundred MB a day, which is where the size limit and its warning matter.

## Acceptance tests

1. With a fake clock, packets received are kept with their times and sources and found again by station and time.
2. Days past **Keep history for** are deleted; past **Use at most**, the oldest days go first, and the status says how
   many days the limit keeps; today's file reaching the limit stops keeping packets for the day, with the warning.
3. After a restart, the stations and tracks of the last `RemoveAfter` are back, in the same state as before, and
   nothing is sent, acked, alerted or counted again.
4. A station's 7-day track is its kept positions with the 20 m rule, at most 10,000 points; **Older packets** pages back
   to the history's start.
5. The dashboard's 7 days range has a point an hour from `metrics.db`; hourly counters older than 90 days are dropped;
   an existing `dashboard-history.json` is imported once.
6. A killed engine keeps every packet committed before the last second; an unreadable day file is set aside and the
   others are read.
7. Off deletes the history after the confirmation and keeps nothing more; Start over deletes it; Clear stations
   doesn't.
8. The gateway's configuration sets the days and size and wins on every start.

## Decisions for review

1. **Persistent station and track history** (maintainer, 2026-10-27), kept as the packets received.
2. **Up to 7 days, configurable, with a size limit defaulting to 1 GB** (maintainer, 2026-10-27).
3. **Warn when the size limit is what's cutting the history short** (maintainer, 2026-10-27).
4. **Longer dashboard ranges that survive upgrades and restarts** (maintainer, 2026-10-27): 7 days now; 30 days can
   follow from the same counters.
5. **Dashboard counters kept for 90 days, hourly**, apart from the packets (maintainer, 2026-10-27).
6. **The map's "stations heard in the last N days"** is later (maintainer, 2026-10-27), as its own issue
   ([#455](https://github.com/APRS-NET/AprsMap/issues/455)); this feature gives it the store it needs.
7. **SQLite, one file a day** (proposed), over one database with deletes (rotation would need `VACUUM`) or append-only
   log files (fast to write but slow to query by station).
8. **Packets as the record** (proposed), stations and tracks rebuilt from them, over keeping station snapshots.
9. **Restore only the last `RemoveAfter`** (proposed), at most 24 hours, so a restart puts back the map as it was and
   memory doesn't grow with the days kept; older history is read when asked for.
10. **Messages stay in their files** (proposed); moving them into the store can follow.
