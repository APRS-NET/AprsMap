# F1.18 Destination

Status: draft

## User story

As an operator heading somewhere (a net control point, a launch site, a station I'm meeting), I set it as my
destination and always see how far it is and in which direction, from my location, with a line to it on the map.

## Behavior

1. **Set destination here** on the map's context menu (right-click or long-press), or **Set as destination** on a
   station (F1.6 actions), or by typing coordinates or a locator in the destination chip.
2. A **destination chip** on the map shows the destination's name (the station's, or its coordinates) with distance
   and bearing from my location (F1.13), in the viewer's units: "W1AW-9 · 42 km NE 038°". Clicking it centres the map
   on the destination; its close button clears it.
3. **On the map**: a flag marker at the destination and a great-circle line from my location to it, in the accent
   colour, dashed.
4. A **station** destination follows the station as it moves.
5. Without my location, the chip shows the destination without distance and bearing, and says "Set your location to
   see the distance".
6. The destination is per viewer (each browser its own), kept across restarts. It changes nothing for the station or
   the filter.

## Service surface

- New `Task<RouteInfo> GetRouteAsync(double lat, double lon)`: distance and bearing from the active profile's location
  (G08), and the great-circle points for the line. Null fields without a location.
- Destination storage is a per-viewer preference (`IPreferenceStore`).

## APRS.NET dependencies

- **G08**: distance, bearing and intermediate great-circle points: delivered (`0.2.0-alpha.2`, `Geodesy`).

## Desktop vs web differences

None.

## Acceptance tests

1. Set destination from the map, a station and typed coordinates or a locator.
2. The chip shows distance and bearing in each unit system and language; a moving station destination updates.
3. The line follows the great circle (a transatlantic destination curves on the Mercator map).
4. Without a location, the hint shows; two browsers keep different destinations.
