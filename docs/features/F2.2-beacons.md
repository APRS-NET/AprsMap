# F2.2 Position and status beacons

Status: built for 0.5.0 ([#196](https://github.com/APRS-NET/AprsMap/issues/196)), on APRS.NET's `AprsNet.Beacons`
(G11, `0.4.0-alpha.3`): one schedule per beacon and source, with the spacing rules, the interlock and the F2.1 layers
on top. Two changes from the draft:

- The settings are in **Settings › Beacons**, for the active profile, rather than a step of the profile editor: they
  sit beside Sources, whose TX enabled decides what Send via lists, and the editor stays a short first-run wizard.
- On a TNC, a decay sequence starts at **10 minutes**, not 1: the 10-minute floor (behavior 6) would hold back every
  shorter step anyway, so the steps are 10, 20, then the interval. APRS-IS keeps 30 seconds, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 minutes.

Moving (speed and turns) isn't offered until GPS (F3.6).

## User story

As a licensed operator, I set my station to beacon its position and a status on its own, choose which of my sources
each beacon goes out on, and see when the next one is due, without flooding the channel or sending anything before I've
checked what it says.

## Background: what already exists

- F2.1: the Transmit switch; Licensed and Disable TX on the profile; TX enabled and the TX path on each source;
  Send position now, on every source that can send or on one; the `PositionConfirmed` interlock, set by the first
  position sent by hand and cleared when the callsign, symbol, comment or location changes; Sent packets in the packet
  list.
- A profile has a fixed location (F0.5, F1.13). A moving station's position comes with GPS (F3.6, 0.6.0).

## Behavior

### The position beacon

1. Each profile has a **position beacon**, **off** for every new and existing profile. Its settings, on the profile
   editor's beacon step:
   1. **Schedule**:
      - **Fixed**: every interval, **30 minutes** by default, from 10 minutes to 24 hours.
      - **Decay after a change** (the default when turned on): a beacon soon after the position, symbol or comment
        changes, then at doubling intervals up to the fixed interval (for example 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, then every 30
        minutes), so a new position spreads quickly and then settles.
      - **Moving (speed and turns)**: SmartBeaconing-style corner pegging (APRS.NET's names and defaults), offered
        once the profile has a GPS (F3.6, 0.6.0); until then shown as coming.
   2. **Send via**: a checkmark for each of the profile's sources that's **TX enabled** (F2.1), all ticked by default.
      Sources that aren't TX enabled aren't listed; the step says how to enable one.
   3. **Format**: uncompressed (the default) or compressed, which is shorter on air.
   4. **PHG** (optional): power, antenna height above average terrain, gain and directivity, from lists of the values
      APRS101 allows, sent as the `PHGphgd` data extension.
2. A beacon is the same position report Send position now sends (F2.1 behavior 10), with the PHG and format above, on
   each Send via source with that source's path.

### The status beacon

3. Each profile has a **status beacon**, off by default: a text of up to 62 characters (APRS101 chapter 16's limit for
   a status without a timestamp), printable ASCII, sent as a `>` status report on a **fixed** interval, **60 minutes**
   by default (10 minutes to 24 hours), with its own **Send via** checkmarks. Saved alternatives come with F2.11.

### When beacons go out

4. Beacons go out only while **every** F2.1 layer allows sending: the Transmit switch is on, the profile is Licensed
   without Disable TX, and the source can send now. A source that can't send now is skipped for that beacon; when none
   can, the beacon is held and the status says why.
5. **The interlock** (F2.1 behavior 12): nothing is beaconed until the profile is `PositionConfirmed`, a position sent by
   hand since the last change to what's sent. Until then the beacon step and the status bar say "Send your position
   once by hand to start beaconing".
6. **Being a good neighbour on RF**: on a TNC, no position beacon goes out within **10 minutes** of the last one on that
   source, whatever the schedule, and a decay sequence starts at 1 minute on RF but at 30 seconds on APRS-IS. Beacons
   that come due together (position and status) are spaced at least **10 seconds** apart.
7. **No catch-up bursts**: after Transmit was off, a connection was down or the app was closed, an overdue beacon goes
   out once, and the schedule carries on from there.
8. **First beacon**: when the beacon is turned on, or the app starts with it on, the first one goes out after a random
   wait of up to the first interval's tenth (at most 3 minutes), so stations that start together don't beacon together.
9. Each beacon is listed in the packet list marked `Sent`, with its source, as F2.1's are.

### Seeing it

10. The status bar's Transmitting marker (F2.1) adds the next beacon's time, "Transmitting · next beacon in 12 min",
    and its tooltip says why that time (interval, decay, waiting for the RF spacing, waiting for the confirmation).
11. The connection menu's **Send position now** stays; sending by hand restarts a decay sequence's count from that send.
12. On a gateway, beacons are the station's: they run from the stored settings, and browsers can't change them until
    F2.9's operator sign-in (the settings gate already applies).

## Service surface

- `StationProfile` gains `PositionBeacon` (`PositionBeaconSettings`: `On`, `Schedule` (`Fixed`, `Decay`, `Moving`),
  `IntervalMinutes`, `SourceIds`, `Compressed`, `Phg`) and `StatusBeacon` (`StatusBeaconSettings`: `On`, `Text`,
  `IntervalMinutes`, `SourceIds`).
- `EngineStatus.Transmit` gains `NextBeacon` (when, which beacon, and the reason) and `BeaconHeld` (why one is waiting).
- Error codes: `settings.profile.beacon-interval-invalid`, `settings.profile.status-invalid`,
  `settings.profile.phg-invalid`.

## Acceptance tests

1. Both beacons are off for new and existing profiles; with them on and the profile not `PositionConfirmed`, nothing is
   sent and the status says why.
2. A fixed 30-minute beacon on APRS-IS and a TNC sends one position report on each every 30 minutes (`FakeTimeProvider`),
   each listed as Sent with its source; a source unticked in Send via gets none.
3. Decay after a location change sends at 30 seconds, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, then every 30 minutes on APRS-IS, and every 10
   minutes then the interval on a TNC.
4. A TNC never gets two position beacons within 10 minutes; position and status due together go out 10 seconds apart.
5. Turning Transmit off holds beacons; turning it on after three missed intervals sends one, not three.
6. PHG and compressed positions encode as APRS.NET writes them; a status of 63 characters, or with a non-ASCII
   character, is refused on save.
7. The status bar shows the next beacon's time and reason.

## Decisions for review

1. **Decay after a change is the default schedule** (proposed): a fixed station's position rarely changes, so it
   settles at the fixed interval, while a change spreads quickly.
2. **30 minutes for positions and 60 for status by default, 10 minutes the shortest** (proposed), the usual fixed-station
   etiquette.
3. **A 10-minute floor per RF source** (proposed), whatever the schedule, as a courtesy to the shared channel; APRS-IS has
   no floor beyond the interval.
4. **One schedule per beacon, Send via picks the sources** (proposed), rather than a schedule per source.
5. **The moving schedule waits for GPS** (proposed): it's shown as coming until F3.6, since a fixed location never moves.
