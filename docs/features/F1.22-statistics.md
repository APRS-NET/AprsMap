# F1.22 Statistics and dashboard

Status: built for 0.4.0-alpha.1 ([#150](https://github.com/APRS-NET/AprsMap/issues/150)), with the extras below in
[#176](https://github.com/APRS-NET/AprsMap/issues/176). Extended 2026-10-16 with
the dashboard (behaviors 1–9) beside the earlier tables (behaviors 10–14). Built: the engine's counters
(`GetDashboardAsync`), and the Dashboard tab beside the map with its range, refresh while shown, and tiles 4.1–4.11 with
their links, the farthest direct hops and weather extremes read from the stations held, in the viewer's units
(behaviors 1–5, 9); the tables of devices, symbols, kinds and reception over the stations held, sorted by count or name,
copied as tab-separated text, each row applying a View filter and showing the map (10–14). A row applies the
nearest condition the View filter has: a device's class, a symbol's category, stations or objects and items, and how
they arrived (decision 9). For 0.5.0 ([#176](https://github.com/APRS-NET/AprsMap/issues/176)): hovering or focusing a sparkline (6) shows the
value and time at that point as a tooltip, with a dot on it, Left and Right (Home, End) stepping through the points;
each tile's **Copy** gives its title, number and each point's time and value as tab-separated text (8); and **My view**
(7): `GetDashboardAsync` takes the viewer's view filter, and the tiles count only the stations it shows (decision 7).

## User story

As an operator watching the feed, I can switch from the map to a **dashboard** of live numbers and sparklines: how
many stations I hear, how many are new, how fast packets arrive, how they reach me, and what's on the air, so I can
see the shape of my area's activity and notice when something changes (a quiet hour, a burst of new stations, a feed
that has gone silent). The tables underneath tell me which devices, programs and symbols stations use.

## Behavior

### Where it lives

1. **A Dashboard tab in the centre, beside the Map.** The centre (F1.0 behavior 1) becomes a tab strip: **Map** and
   **Dashboard**. Map stays first and can't be closed; Dashboard can be closed and reopened from the app menu (Show
   Dashboard) like a panel. Ctrl+Tab reaches it. The side panels (Stations, Details, Packets) stay where they are, so
   selecting a station on the dashboard still fills Details. Narrow windows (F1.0 behavior 14) get a Dashboard tab
   beside Map, Stations, Details and Packets. The open tab is part of the saved layout (F1.0, F1.23).
2. **Numbers update while the tab is shown**, every 10 seconds, and stop when it's hidden (the map keeps its own
   updates). The time of the numbers is shown ("as of 14:32:10").
3. **A time range** at the top: **1 hour · 6 hours · 24 hours** (default 1 hour), per viewer. Sparklines cover the
   range: one point a minute for 1 hour, every 5 minutes for 6 hours, every 15 minutes for 24 hours. Before the
   engine has been running for the whole range, the sparklines start where its history starts and say so ("since
   06:01").

### Tiles

4. **Tiles** in a grid that reflows with the window's width (four across when wide, one across on a phone). Each tile
   has a title, a big number, a short comparison, and a sparkline over the range, drawn from theme tokens like F1.11's
   telemetry sparklines (the same drawing). The number is always written, never shown only as a line; each tile reads
   to a screen reader as "Packets received, 1,240 in the last hour, 22 a minute now".
   1. **Stations heard**: distinct stations heard in the range; the sparkline is distinct stations per point. The
      comparison is the count in the store now (F1.3) and how many are faded (F1.4 aging).
   2. **New stations**: stations heard within the range that hadn't been heard in the 24 hours before (or since the
      engine started, or since packets were last cleared, F1.19); the sparkline is new stations per point. Below the number, the five newest, each a link
      that selects the station.
   3. **Packets received**: total in the range and the rate now (per minute, over the last 5 minutes); the sparkline
      is packets per point. With several sources (F1.25), a line per source in its own token colour and a legend.
   4. **How packets arrive** (F1.21, G20): heard directly on RF, through digipeaters, and from the internet only, as
      percentages with a small stacked bar, and a sparkline of the RF share.
   5. **Packet types**: positions, weather, messages, status, telemetry, objects and items, other, as a stacked bar with
      counts.
   6. **Problems**: invalid packets (F1.20) and lines rejected, with a sparkline; the tile links to the packet list with
      Issues only on (F1.8).
   7. **Connection** (F1.1, F1.25): each source's state, time connected, reconnects in the range, and time since the
      last packet. A source silent for more than 2 minutes while connected is marked "quiet", in words and with the
      warning token.
   8. **Busiest stations**: the ten that sent the most packets in the range, with counts and a bar; a row selects the
      station.
   9. **Busiest digipeaters and IGates** (F1.21 links): the ten that relayed or gated the most packets in the range.
   10. **Farthest heard directly**: the five longest direct RF hops in the range (station, IGate, distance in the
       viewer's units), from F1.21's routes.
   11. **Weather** (F1.10): weather stations reporting in the range, and the lowest and highest temperature and the
       strongest gust among them, each naming its station.
5. **Clicking a tile's number or row** goes somewhere useful: a station row selects it (and Details shows it); the
   arrival and type bars apply a view filter (F1.9) and switch to the Map; Problems opens the packet list with Issues
   only on.
6. **Hovering or focusing a sparkline** shows the value and time at that point (a tooltip; with the keyboard, Left and
   Right move along it).
   1. **Expand** on each tile with a sparkline (maintainer request, 2026-10-25) opens its series as a large plot over
      the tiles, below the range and My view switches, which still work: a value axis with a few round ticks, a time
      axis labelled at even steps, faint grid lines, and the line, broken where a point has no value. A **cursor**, a
      vertical line with a dot on the nearest point, follows the pointer; Left and Right (Home, End) move it with the
      keyboard. A tooltip at the cursor and a readout above the plot say that point's value and time ("12 at 14:05"),
      the readout read by a screen reader. The plot updates with the tiles every 10 seconds and keeps its cursor
      where it is. **Close** or Escape returns to the tiles, with focus back on Expand; Copy works as on the tile.
7. **Respects the view filter, if asked**: a **Whole feed · My view** switch (default Whole feed). With My view, the
   tiles count only stations the viewer's view filter (F1.9) shows, in points of at least 5 minutes. Packets by source,
   the busiest digipeaters and IGates, and each source's last packet stay the whole feed's, and a note under the switch
   says so; with the view filter off, My view says it's counting the whole feed (decision 7).
8. **Copy** on each tile copies its numbers and series as tab-separated text, for a spreadsheet.
9. **Nothing to show**: with no packets yet, tiles show zeros and "Waiting for packets", not empty boxes.

### Tables (the earlier Statistics page, now under the tiles)

10. Below the tiles, the tables over the stations currently in the store (F1.3):
    1. **Platforms**: device vendor and model (G07), grouped by class (tracker, rig, software, digipeater, other), with
       counts and percentages;
    2. **Symbols**: each symbol with its drawing, name, count and percentage;
    3. **Kinds**: stations, objects, items;
    4. **Reception** (G20): RF direct, RF via digipeaters, APRS-IS only.
11. Tables sort by any column and are accessible tables (headers, row and column names for screen readers); each has
    a compact bar for the percentage, drawn from theme tokens with the number always written.
12. **Clicking a row** applies it as a view filter (F1.9) and switches to the Map, so "show me every APRSdroid
    station" is one click.
13. Tables update with the tiles (behavior 2).
14. **Copy** copies a table as tab-separated text.

## Engine

- The engine keeps **per-minute counters for the last 24 hours** (1,440 buckets, a fixed-size ring, reused as time
  moves on): packets by source, by arrival kind and by type; invalid packets and rejected lines; new stations. Coarser
  points (5 and 15 minutes) are summed from the minutes.
- **Distinct stations** are counted a point for each point size (1, 5 and 15 minutes), each in its own ring: a station
  counts once in a point however many packets it sends, because the engine remembers the last point it was counted
  in.
- **Per-station and per-relay counts** are kept in 5-minute steps for the 24 hours, so the busiest lists for the last
  hour cover 60 to 65 minutes. Stations and relays not heard for 24 hours are dropped once a minute, so memory follows
  the feed.
- Each source's last packet time is kept, including a source that only repeated what another delivered first
  (F1.25 behavior 9), so a working source isn't marked quiet.
- The farthest direct hops and the weather extremes are read from the station store (F1.3) when the dashboard is
  asked for, not counted.
- The counters live in the engine, not the viewer, so on a gateway every viewer sees the same history, including what
  arrived before they opened the page, and it costs the same with one viewer or fifty.
- Clearing data (F1.19) clears the counters with the packet history. The counters aren't saved across restarts
  (decision 3).

## Service surface

- New `Task<Dashboard> GetDashboardAsync(DashboardRange range)`: `Dashboard` holds the time, where the history
  starts, the first point's time and the step, each tile's series (`IReadOnlyList<int>`, a value a point) and totals,
  the rate now, the newest stations, the top lists and each source's last packet. Read-only, so open to every viewer on
  a gateway. Its `StationFilter? filter` argument is My view (behavior 7): null or empty for the whole feed, and
  `Dashboard.MyView` says when it was used.
- It replaces the earlier draft's `GetStatisticsAsync`; the tables share the facet counts with F1.9's
  `GetViewFacetsAsync`.
- The UI polls it every 10 seconds while the tab is shown (behavior 2); a later `WatchDashboardAsync` stream can
  replace polling if the gateway's load calls for it.

## APRS.NET dependencies

- **G07, device identification** and **G20, path analysis**: delivered (`0.2.0-alpha.2`, `AprsNet.DeviceId` and
  `PathAnalysis`).
- Packet types come from the parsed packet kinds the engine already uses. No new gaps.

## Acceptance tests

1. A scripted feed on a fake clock fills the minute counters; each tile's number and series are right for 1, 6 and 24
   hours, and buckets older than 24 hours fall off.
2. New stations count first hearings only; a station heard again isn't new; clearing data starts the count over.
3. Packets per source split correctly with two sources; arrival shares add up to 100 %.
4. A connected source with no packet for 2 minutes is marked quiet; a packet clears it.
5. The tab polls only while shown (a fake engine counts calls); hiding it stops polling.
6. My view counts only stations the view filter shows.
7. Clicking a busiest-station row selects it; clicking an arrival bar sets the view filter and shows the Map.
8. Each tile's screen-reader text names its number and range; sparkline points are reachable by keyboard.
9. Every palette and theme at 200 % text: tiles reflow without clipping; one column at phone width.
10. Tables: counts and percentages add up to the total; sorting, accessible names and copy format are right; a
    platform row sets the view filter (the earlier acceptance tests 1–4).
11. Expand: a tile's plot shows its title, number and series; moving the pointer or pressing Left, Right, Home and End
    moves the cursor and its readout says the point's value and time; Escape closes it and focus returns to Expand;
    it fits at 200 % text in every theme.

## Decisions for review

1. **A centre tab, not a panel** (maintainer request, 2026-10-16): the dashboard takes the map's space when shown,
   which suits watching numbers full-size; the side panels stay. The alternative is a dockable panel like Packets,
   which could sit beside the map but would be cramped.
2. **Ranges of 1, 6 and 24 hours** (proposed), with points at 1, 5 and 15 minutes. Longer ranges need saved history.
3. **History isn't saved across restarts** (proposed): counters start with the engine. Saving them (a small file per
   day) is a later addition if a week view is wanted.
4. **Whole feed by default** (proposed), with My view one click away, so the numbers describe the feed the station
   receives.
5. **Quiet after 2 minutes** (proposed): a connected APRS-IS feed with a range filter normally sees a packet well
   within that.
6. **Polling every 10 seconds** (proposed): cheap for one summary call, and the tiles don't need second-by-second
   changes.
7. **My view from per-station 5-minute tallies** (built that way, maintainer's choice 2026-10-18): each station's
   5-minute steps keep a count per way its packets arrived and kind, with problems, so a filter's numbers are summed
   from the stations it shows. That costs a few bytes a station a step, and My view's points are at least 5 minutes.
   Packets by source and the busiest relays aren't kept per station, so they stay the whole feed's.
8. **"New" means not heard in the previous 24 hours** (built that way), since the engine keeps 24 hours of stations;
   a station back after a day away counts as new again.
9. **Table rows filter by the nearest View filter condition** (built that way): a device row by its class, a symbol
   row by its category, since the View filter has no device-model or single-symbol condition. Adding those would make
   "every APRSdroid station" exact.
10. **The expanded plot is an overlay inside the dashboard** (proposed), not a dialog or a window: it works the same
    when the dashboard is floated in its own window or in a browser, and the range switches stay in reach.
