# F2.3 Where others see you

Status: built for 0.6.0 ([#197](https://github.com/APRS-NET/AprsMap/issues/197)); decisions proposed 2026-10-21 for the
maintainer's review. In the UI: a ghost of the station on the map, and the Transmit pill's tooltip.

## User story

As a mobile operator, I see where other stations think I am, from my last beacon moved on along its course and speed as
their maps would, against where my GPS says I really am, so I know how far off they are and whether a beacon is due.

## Background: what already exists

- GPS (F3.6): while its fix is fresh, the station is where the GPS says.
- Beacons (F2.2) and Send position now (F2.1) send the position, with the GPS's course and speed.
- APRS.NET's `DeadReckoning` (G11, delivered in 0.4.0-alpha.12): from a position, course, speed and time, where a station
  probably is later, and how far that is from where it really is (`ErrorAt`).
- Dead reckoning for other stations (F1.17) draws where they probably are.
- Observed in APRSIS32 (docs/reference/functional-inventory.md): where others think the station is, from its last
  beaconed course and speed; a beacon when that's off by more than a set distance, not before a minimum interval; and
  an on-screen indicator of the error's size and direction. This design and its name are the app's own.

## Behavior

1. **Where others see you**: after each position this station sends, the engine keeps it with its course and speed.
   Where others see the station now is that position moved on along the course at the speed for the time since, as
   APRS.NET's dead reckoning works it out; a position sent without course and speed stays where it was.
2. It's shown **only while the GPS has a fresh fix** (F3.6) (proposed): without one, the app doesn't know where the
   station really is, so it can't say how far off others are.
3. **On the map**: a ring in the accent colour where others see the station, joined to the station's house by a
   dashed line, while they're more than **50 m** apart (proposed; GPS noise is about that).
4. **The Transmit pill's tooltip** adds "Others see you 1.2 km from here" (or "where you are" under 50 m).
5. It lasts until the next position is sent, or **30 minutes** after the last (proposed): after that, other stations'
   maps have mostly stopped moving it on (F1.17's 10 minutes is the app's own).
6. Indicator only (proposed): beacons aren't triggered by the distance; the moving schedule (F2.2, #335) beacons by speed
   and turns. A beacon when the error passes a set distance, not before a minimum interval, is a later choice
   (APRS.NET's schedules take it as `Drifted()`).

## Service surface

- `TransmitStatus.SeenAt` (`SeenPosition`): where others see the station (latitude, longitude), how far that is from
  where it is in km, and when the position they have was sent; null when not shown (behavior 2, 5).

## APRS.NET dependencies

G11 (`DeadReckoning`, delivered in 0.4.0-alpha.12).

## Desktop vs web differences

None: the engine works it out, every viewer sees it.

## Acceptance tests

1. After a beacon at 60 knots east, 2 minutes later with the GPS still where the beacon was, others see the station
   about 3.7 km east, and the status says so.
2. With the GPS moving on at the same speed and course, the distance stays near 0.
3. A position sent without course and speed is where others see it.
4. Without a fresh GPS fix, or 30 minutes after the last position, nothing is shown.
5. The map draws the ring and line beyond 50 m; the tooltip says the distance.

## Decisions for review

All proposed (2026-10-21), built as described so they can be tried:

1. **Only with a fresh GPS fix.**
2. **Shown beyond 50 m**; the tooltip says it always.
3. **30 minutes** after the last position, then nothing.
4. **An indicator only**, no beacon triggered by the distance.
5. **The name**: "Where others see you", rather than a coloured dot.
